NEW YORK · REVIEWED NOVEMBER 23, 2026

A journal with a purpose.

Recordkeeping support based on 19 NYCRR Part 182.

What to record

Section 182.9 requires records made contemporaneously with the act: the date and approximate time; type and number of services; each individual's name and address; identity credential type and applicable witness names and credentials; and verification procedures. Electronic acts also need communication technology and, where separate, certification authority and verification provider details.

Keep and produce the record

Retain records for at least ten years and be able to produce them to the Secretary of State and others as necessary for your legal obligations. Third-party storage must be protected by a password or another secure access method. Ledger has no deletion feature and conservatively measures retention from the later of entry or act date.

Paper records and signatures

Digitization preserves a copy; it cannot make a historical record contemporaneous. Preserve paper originals until their disposal has been separately reviewed. Section 182.9 does not expressly require a principal's journal signature, thumbprint, full credential number, or copy of the notarized document. This app does not collect those by default.

Electronic notarization has additional duties

Section 182.8 requires audio/video recording, retention for at least ten years from the transaction, and limits on recorded personally identifiable information. Executive Law §135-c and Part 182 also govern registration, identity verification, electronic signatures, and communication technology. A digital journal alone does not satisfy those requirements.
